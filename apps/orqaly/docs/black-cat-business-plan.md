## 1. Executive Summary

**Melnais Kakis** (Latvian for "Black Cat") is a direct-to-consumer premium cat food brand targeting Latvia's 296,000+ cat-owning households through a branded e-commerce store. The business sources private-label premium and super-premium cat food from established EU manufacturers, packages it under a distinctive black cat identity rooted in Latvian folklore (where black cats are symbols of prosperity and harvest abundance), and sells direct to consumers via parcel locker delivery [16][17].

### The Opportunity in Numbers

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Latvia cat population | ~407,000 | Statista 2023 [16] |
| Cat-owning households | ~296,000 (37% of ~800,000 households) | FEDIAF/Statista [16] |
| Latvia household cat ownership rate | 37% (vs. 27% EU average) | FEDIAF [16] |
| Global pet food market (2025) | USD 128.7 billion | Grand View Research [1] |
| Cat food global CAGR (to 2033) | 5.1% | Grand View Research [1] |
| European pet food market (2025) | USD 57.33 billion | Future Market Insights [4] |
| Cat food as growth driver | CAGR 4% to 2030 | Euromonitor [17][18] |

### Why This Works

1. **No Latvian-born premium cat food brand exists.** BioRaw serves a raw-food niche only. Every premium option is a foreign import with generic branding.
2. **Direct-to-consumer model captures full margin.** Competitors distribute through retail intermediaries (Maxima, Rimi, pet shops), losing 30-50% of retail margin to the channel.
3. **Latvia's 0% corporate income tax on reinvested profits** means all growth-phase profits reinvested in inventory, marketing, and expansion are tax-free [27].
4. **Cultural resonance:** In Latvian folklore, black cats (associated with the harvest spirit Rungis) symbolize prosperity and abundance - a unique, authentic brand story no competitor can replicate.

## 2. Market Analysis

### 2.1 Global & European Context

The global pet food market was valued at USD 128.7 billion in 2025, projected to reach USD 191.2 billion by 2033 at a CAGR of 5.1% [1]. The European pet food market reached USD 57.33 billion in 2025, growing to an estimated USD 60.41 billion in 2026 [4]. Cat food specifically is forecast to grow at a CAGR of 4% to 2030, driven by what Euromonitor calls "feline favouritism" - the global shift in pet care spending toward cats [17][18].

Two structural trends underpin this growth:

- **Humanization:** Pet owners increasingly view cats as family members, driving demand for higher-quality, ingredient-transparent food [17]
- **Premiumization:** Willingness to pay more for grain-free, high-protein, and "natural" formulations is rising across all European markets [4]

### 2.2 Latvia Pet Market Specifics

| Metric | Value | Confidence | Source |
|--------|-------|------------|--------|
| Cat population | ~407,000 | High | Statista 2023 [16] |

| Household cat ownership rate | 37% | High | FEDIAF - 3rd highest in EU [16] |

| Estimated total pet food market | EUR 50-85 million | Low-Medium | Cross-referenced estimate (see note) |

**Note on market size:** Some reports cite Latvia's pet food market at EUR 1.3-1.5 million, which likely represents a narrow sub-segment (e.g., premium dry food only or private-label only). Cross-referencing Latvia's cat population of 407,000 with average annual per-cat food spend across Central/Eastern Europe (EUR 120-200), the total cat food market alone would be EUR 49-81 million. Statista's market volume forecast of USD 84.99 million by 2027 aligns with this range. We use EUR 60 million as a working estimate for total pet food, of which cat food represents approximately 40-45% (EUR 24-27 million).

### 2.4 The Black Cat Cultural Advantage

In Latvian folklore, black cats are symbols of prosperity. Latvian farmers associated black cats in grain silos with the harvest spirit Rungis - a deity of abundance. A group of black cats signaled a good harvest. Farmers kept black cats to protect against evil spirits and ensure food for winter. This is a living cultural tradition, not an import. No competitor can credibly claim this positioning.

Brand implication: "Melnais Kakis" is not just a name - it is a culturally authentic story that provides premium pricing justification, emotional resonance, and social-media-native shareability.

- **What we do NOT compete on:** Manufacturing scale, ingredient science, veterinary endorsements

## 7. Payment Processing Setup

### 7.1 Processor Comparison

| Feature | Montonio | Stripe | Kevin |
|---------|----------|--------|-------|
| Latvia support | Yes (Baltic specialist) | Yes (full Eurozone) | Yes (Baltic focus) |
| Card fees (EU) | ~0.79% + EUR 0.10 (starting tier) | 1.4% + EUR 0.25 [29] | Not publicly listed |
| Bank link fees | EUR 0.10 per transaction (fixed) | Not native | Fixed fee per transaction |
| Monthly fee | Subscription tiers available | None | None (usage-based) |
| BNPL | Built-in (EUR 30-2,500) | Via Klarna/Afterpay add-on | No |
| Latvian bank links | Direct (Swedbank, SEB, Citadele, Luminor) | Requires add-on | Direct bank integration |
| Payout speed | 1-3 business days | 2-7 business days | 1-2 business days |
| Subscription billing | Basic | Mature (Stripe Billing) | No |
| Setup complexity | Low (Baltic-optimized plugins) | Medium (well-documented APIs) | Medium |

### 7.2 Recommended Setup

**Primary: Montonio** for bank link payments and card payments at launch. Latvian consumers strongly prefer bank link payments over cards [27][30]. Montonio's direct integration with all major Latvian banks (Swedbank, SEB, Citadele, Luminor) and fixed EUR 0.10 per bank link transaction makes it the clear choice for domestic sales.

**Secondary: Stripe** for subscription billing (recurring payments), international card payments, and future expansion to Estonia/Lithuania. Add at Month 3-4 when subscription model launches. Standard EU card fee: 1.4% + EUR 0.25 [29].

**Kevin: Monitor only.** Potentially lower bank-to-bank fees, but limited pricing transparency and regulatory concerns make it risky for a startup. Revisit at Month 12+.

| Shipping to customer (single shipment) | EUR 3.25 |
| Order packaging | EUR 0.65 |
| **Total cost to serve** | **EUR 13.58** |
| **Gross profit per order** | **EUR 14.22** |
| **Gross margin** | **51.2%** |

**Why this differs from single-item margins:** Shipping and packaging are fixed costs per order, not per item. Multi-item orders amortize these costs, pushing effective margins from 38-46% (single item) to 51%+ (multi-item). This is why the free shipping threshold at EUR 30 is critical - it incentivizes multi-item orders where unit economics are strongest.

### 9.5 Key Unit Economics Observations

1. **Dry food is the margin engine.** 2 kg bags: 39-46% margin. 6 kg bags: 55-57% margin. Prioritize dry food as the hero SKU.
2. **Wet food is a retention driver, not a profit center.** 21-30% margins are thin. Use wet food as a subscription add-on and cross-sell, not as a standalone acquisition product.
3. **6 kg bags are the most profitable SKU.** 55-57% gross margin. Push value-size through subscription bundles.
4. **Shipping is the margin killer on small orders.** EUR 3.25 on a EUR 11.90 order is 27% of revenue. On a EUR 39.90 order, it is only 8%.
5. **Multi-item orders transform the economics.** Gross margin jumps from ~40% (single item) to ~51% (multi-item) purely through shipping amortization.

## 11. Sensitivity Analysis & Break-Even

### 11.1 Single-Variable Sensitivity (Base Case Year 1)

The table below shows how Year 1 net contribution changes when a single assumption shifts by +/-20% while all others stay at base case:

| Variable | -20% | Base | +20% | Impact Range | Verdict |
|----------|------|------|------|-------------|---------|
| Average Order Value | EUR 8,050 | EUR 16,608 | EUR 25,166 | EUR 17,116 | **High sensitivity** |
| Order Volume | EUR 4,862 | EUR 16,608 | EUR 28,354 | EUR 23,492 | **Highest sensitivity** |
| Gross Margin | EUR 3,504 | EUR 16,608 | EUR 29,712 | EUR 26,208 | **Highest sensitivity** |
| Customer Acquisition Cost | EUR 18,588 | EUR 16,608 | EUR 14,628 | EUR 3,960 | Low sensitivity |
| Shipping Cost per Order | EUR 20,088 | EUR 16,608 | EUR 13,128 | EUR 6,960 | Medium sensitivity |

**Interpretation:** The model is most sensitive to gross margin and order volume. A 20% drop in gross margin (from 40% to 32%) reduces Year 1 net contribution by 79%. A 20% drop in order volume reduces it by 71%. However, even at -20% on the most sensitive variable, the business remains close to break-even rather than deeply unprofitable. CAC and shipping costs matter less because they are smaller components of total cost.

### 11.3 Break-Even Analysis

#### Monthly Operating Break-Even

Fixed monthly costs at base case:
- Marketing: EUR 500-800/month (average EUR 600)
- Fixed OpEx (tools, hosting, insurance): EUR 250-400/month (average EUR 325)
- **Total fixed monthly costs: EUR 925**

Contribution margin per order (at AOV EUR 30, 40% gross margin):
- Gross profit per order: EUR 12.00
- Less: CAC amortized per order (EUR 10 / 1.5 orders per acquired customer): EUR 6.67
- **Net contribution per order: EUR 5.33**

**Monthly break-even: EUR 925 / EUR 5.33 = ~174 orders/month**

However, this is the fully-loaded break-even including new customer acquisition costs. For orders from existing/returning customers (no CAC), contribution per order is EUR 12.00, requiring only 77 orders/month to cover fixed costs.

**Blended break-even (assuming 40% repeat customers by Month 6): ~110-130 orders/month**

### 11.4 What Must Be True for This Business to Work

The financial model rests on five assumptions that must hold simultaneously. If any one of them breaks badly, the model fails:

1. **Supplier COGS must be at or below EUR 3.00/kg for standard dry food and EUR 5.00/kg for premium dry food.** If COGS runs 30%+ above these benchmarks, gross margins drop below 30% and the business cannot cover acquisition costs.

2. **Average order value must reach EUR 25+ by Month 3.** The free shipping threshold at EUR 30 is designed to push this. If AOV stays below EUR 20, shipping costs consume margins entirely.

3. **Customer acquisition cost must stay below EUR 15.** At EUR 20+ CAC, the business needs 14+ months of customer retention to break even on each cohort. In a market with 8-12% monthly churn, that is untenable.

4. **Subscription conversion must reach 20%+ by Month 6.** Subscription revenue provides the recurring base that funds growth. Without it, the business is trapped in a perpetual acquisition-spend cycle.

5. **Shipping costs must not exceed EUR 4.00 per parcel locker delivery.** Omniva's current pricing (EUR 2.69-3.99) supports this. If carrier pricing increases materially, renegotiate or switch carriers.
