In Estonia, pet food is classified and regulated legally as **animal feed** rather than human food. The regulatory framework is heavily harmonized at the European Union level and enforced nationally by Estonia's **Agriculture and Food Board** (*Põllumajandus- ja Toiduamet* or PTA) under the Ministry of Regional Affairs and Agriculture [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>) [3](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/general-information>). Additional oversight regarding consumer protection and imports is carried out alongside the Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority (*TTJA*) and the Estonian Tax and Customs Board [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>) [6](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/doing-business/enterprise-in-the-european-union/technical-regulations-for-products>).

The primary regulatory requirements for distributing, manufacturing, or selling pet food in Estonia fall into several key areas:

---

### 1. Feed Business Operator (FBO) Registration and Approval
Under the **Estonian Feed Act** (*Söödaseadus*) and **Regulation (EC) No 183/2005** (Feed Hygiene Regulation), any entity handling animal feed must be registered or approved before operating: [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>) [7](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/licences-and-notices-of-economic-activity/agriculture/notice-of-economic-activities-for-feed-business-operators>) [8](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/licences-and-notices-of-economic-activity/food/food-handling-activity-licence>)
* **Notice of Economic Activity (*majandustegevusteade*):** Wholesalers, retailers, distributors, transporters, and storage facilities handling pet food must submit a notice of economic activity to the PTA via the Estonian state portal (*Eesti.ee*) or the Ministry's client portal [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>) [7](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/licences-and-notices-of-economic-activity/agriculture/notice-of-economic-activities-for-feed-business-operators>) [8](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/licences-and-notices-of-economic-activity/food/food-handling-activity-licence>).
* **Activity Licence / Facility Approval (*tegevusluba / tunnustamine*):** Businesses that manufacture pet food—especially those utilizing animal by-products—or manufacture certain feed additives and premixes must apply for an official activity licence from the PTA [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>) [4](<https://pta.agri.ee/media/4421/download>) [7](<https://www.eesti.ee/eraisik/en/artikkel/licences-and-notices-of-economic-activity/agriculture/notice-of-economic-activities-for-feed-business-operators>). The facility must undergo an on-site compliance inspection before approval is granted [5](<https://www.ages.at/en/animal/feed/manufacturing-placing-on-the-market/pet-food-production>).

---

### 2. Sourcing and Animal By-Product (ABP) Compliance
If the pet food contains meat, fish, or any animal-derived ingredients, it must adhere strictly to **Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009** and **Regulation (EU) No 142/2011** regarding animal by-products:
* **Category 3 Materials Only:** Only Category 3 materials (derived from animals slaughtered in an approved slaughterhouse and certified fit for human consumption, or wild game hunted for human consumption) can be used to manufacture pet food. Fallen livestock, condemned material, or catering waste (swill) are prohibited.
* **Processing and Hygiene Standards:** Commercial processed pet food must undergo standardized heat treatment (e.g., reaching at least 90°C throughout) or an officially validated equivalent method to ensure microbiological safety [4](<https://pta.agri.ee/media/4421/download>).
* **Microbiological Criteria:** Batches must meet statutory safety criteria, including the verifiable absence of *Salmonella* and acceptable limits for *Enterobacteriaceae* [4](<https://pta.agri.ee/media/4421/download>).

---

### 3. Food Safety Management and HACCP
Under Regulation (EC) No 183/2005, pet food operators must establish and maintain a documented **self-control plan (*enesekontrolliplaan*)** based on the principles of **HACCP** (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points): [5](<https://www.ages.at/en/animal/feed/manufacturing-placing-on-the-market/pet-food-production>)
* Facilities must manage cross-contamination risks, implement pest control, and maintain hygiene standards for equipment, personnel, and packaging materials [5](<https://www.ages.at/en/animal/feed/manufacturing-placing-on-the-market/pet-food-production>).
* **Traceability:** In compliance with General Food Law (Regulation (EC) No 178/2002), businesses must ensure complete "one step back, one step forward" traceability [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>). Detailed lot registers of all suppliers, shipments, raw ingredients, and customer distributions must be maintained.

---

### 4. Packaging and Labelling Requirements
Pet food placed on the Estonian market must conform to **Regulation (EC) No 767/2009** (Placing on the market and use of feed) and Estonian language requirements:
* **Language:** In accordance with the Estonian Language Act and Consumer Protection Act, all mandatory labelling details must appear in **Estonian**. A compliant Estonian-language sticker translation placed over existing packaging is acceptable.
* **Mandatory Label Information:**
  * **Type of Feed:** Must clearly designate whether it is a "Complete feed" (*täissööt*) or "Complementary feed" (*täiendsööt* / e.g., treats).
  * **Target Species & Life Stage:** The specific animal species and categories intended for consumption (e.g., "Complete feed for adult cats").
  * **Directions for Use:** Feeding instructions, including recommended daily intake amounts and instructions to provide fresh water.
  * **Composition (Ingredients):** Feed materials must be listed in descending order by weight, either by specific ingredient names or by approved functional categories (e.g., "meat and animal derivatives").
  * **Analytical Constituents:** Crude protein, crude fat, crude fibre, crude ash, and moisture content (if exceeding statutory limits).
  * **Feed Additives:** Approved vitamins, preservatives, trace elements, or flavorings added (pursuant to Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003) must be listed with their names or identification codes where required [1](<https://foodhealthlegal.eu/>).
  * **Responsible EU Operator:** The business name and address of the manufacturer or the EU-established business responsible for the marketing.
  * **Traceability and Expiration:** Net quantity, batch/lot reference number, and minimum durability date ("Best before").
  * **Establishment Approval/Registration Number:** Where applicable, the official registration/approval code of the manufacturing facility [5](<https://www.ages.at/en/animal/feed/manufacturing-placing-on-the-market/pet-food-production>).

---

### 5. Marketing Claims and Nutritional Declarations
The PTA and TTJA strictly enforce feed marketing standards under Regulation (EC) No 767/2009:
* Claims regarding specific qualities, grain-free formulas, or ingredients must be scientifically substantiated, objective, and verifiable.
* **Prohibition of Medicinal Claims:** Pet food cannot claim to prevent, treat, or cure diseases (e.g., "cures arthritis"), as these are reserved for registered veterinary medicinal products.
* **Dietetic Feeds (PARNUT):** Only feeds intended for particular nutritional purposes (e.g., reduction of struvite stone recurrence, support of renal function) may make specific health-support claims, and they must comply with EU PARNUT regulations and compositional requirements.

---

### 6. Importation from Non-EU Countries (Third Countries)
If bringing pet food into Estonia from outside the European Economic Area:
* The manufacturing facility must be located in an authorized third country and listed as an **approved EU-export establishment** in the European Commission's TRACES NT system.
* Shipments must enter through a designated **Border Control Post (BCP)** accompanied by an official veterinary health certificate.
* Mandatory documentary, identity, and physical veterinary inspections must be cleared with the PTA border veterinary service before release for free circulation by the Tax and Customs Board [2](<https://pta.agri.ee/en/animals/feed-control>).
